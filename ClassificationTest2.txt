Business Summary Sep 01, 2023 CORPORATE OVERVIEW. Broadcom (AVGO) is a leading designer, developer, and global supplier of a broad range of analog semiconductor devices, with a focus on radio frequency (RF) and optoelectronic applications. AVGO breaks its revenue into two primary segments: Semiconductor Solutions (78% of FY 22 sales) and Infrastructure Software (22% of FY 22 sales). The company strategically focuses its research and development resources to address niche opportunities in its target markets and leverage its extensive portfolio of the U.S. and other patents, and other intellectual property (IP) to integrate multiple technologies and create system-on-chip (SoC) component and software olutions that target growth opportunities. AVGO develops semiconductor devices with a focus on comple\\U203a igital and mixed signal complementary metal oxide semiconductor (CMOS) based devices and analog III- based products. Semiconductor solutions manage the movement of data in data center, telecom, enterprise, and embedded networking applications. AVGO provides a broad variety of radio frequency semiconductor devices, wireless connectivity solutions, and custom touch controllers for mobile applications. The company also provides emiconductor solutions for enabling the set-top box and broadband access markets, and for enablin ecure movement of digital data to and from host machines, such as servers, personal computers, an storage systems, to the underlying storage devices, such as hard disk drives and solid state drives. AVGO supports the wireless industry with a broad variety of RF semiconductor devices. It was among the first to deliver commercial film bulk acoustic resonator, or FBAR, filters, which offer technological advantages over competing filters for smartphones to function more efficiently in today's congested RF spectrum. FBAR technology historically has maintained a significant market share within code division multiple access, or CDMA. In the storage and Ethernet networking markets, AVGO supplies transceivers that receive and transmit information along optical fibers. In Ethernet networking, AVGO focuses on higher bandwidth applications for data centers as well as enterprise customers. In storage, it focuses on fiber channel-based applications. Broadcom also sells communication solutions for set-top box, cable modem, and carrier access. Enterprise storage products enable secure movement of digital data to and from host machines such as servers, personal computers, and storage systems to the underlying storage devices such as hard disk drives and solid state drives. Industrial and automotive products include optical isolators, or optocouplers, which provide electrical insulation and signal isolation for signaling systems that are susceptible to electrical noise or interference. The Infrastructure software segment offers a portfolio of mainframe, enterprise, and storage area networking solutions, which enables customers to utilize the benefits of agility, automation, insights, resiliency, and security in managing business processes and technology investments, and to reduce the cost and complexity of managing business information within a shared data storage environment. AVGO's mainframe software solutions include solutions for the IBM Z mainframe platform, which runs its customers' mission-critical business applications. These software products help customers improve economics by increasing throughput and lowering cost per transaction, increasing business agility through DevOps tooling and processes, increasing reliability and availability of operations through machine intelligence and automation solutions, and protecting enterprise data with security and compliance. The company's enterprise software solutions enable large global organizations to transform into digital businesses by providing a digital infrastructure management platform that delivers speed, agility, and the ability to optimize for risk across multi-cloud hybrid environments. CFRA Sub-Industry Outlook Industry Performance We have a positive fundamental outlook for the semiconductors sub-industry over the next 12 months. We expect semiconductor sales to rebound about 10% in 2024 after our forecast for a 12% decline in 2023, with a cyclical recovery to start in the second half of the year (Y/Y revenue growth returns in Q4 2023]. We are starting to see a return to sequential growth across the more cyclical areas of the semiconductor market (PCs, smartphones, and gaming), driven in part by seasonality as well as a more normalized inventory channel after sharply undershipping end-demand for the last several quarters. Whether or not recent strength can be sustained will be dependent on macro factors and the health of the consumer, but we think the industry is in a very healthy situation. Currently, the biggest question mark is within the industrial/automotive markets as those areas held up well through 1H 2023, but channel inventory is now being rightsized due to end-market softness, which may continue until at least Q1 2024. Although inventories are still above optimal levels, companies are making efforts (e.g., production cuts) to quickly get back to normalized levels. In the long term, we expect content growth in automobiles to sharply outpace semiconductor industry growth, driven by several megatrends, such as autonomy, electrification, and connected infotainment. Analog semiconductors are particularly exposed to these trends. Although data center spending has been relatively muted in 2023 (we estimate -2%), we see a 10%- 15% rebound in 2024, with investments continuing to be focused on upgrading the network to meet more artificial intelligence (Al) driven workloads. As a result, we expect accelerated computing technologies (GPUs, FPGAs, and ASICs) to gain traction, in our view, as they help overcome the limitations of CPUs. We believe that the recent spend shift towards accelerators will be permanent and will grow to an annual run rate of over $130 billion by 2027 versus our estimated $45 billion-$50 billion run rate in 2023. Chipmakers tied to the data center are exposed to the best financially positioned customers on the planet, and despite quarterly lumpiness, the long-term prospects related to themes like Al and the metaverse remain extremely encouraging and offer significant growth potential ahead. We believe the semiconductor industry will witness a super cycle over the next decade as content growth per device across a host of markets accelerates, driven by the emergence of greater Al capabilities. Given this, we estimate the semiconductors industry will grow at a 10-year annualized pace of 6%-7% (using 2021 expected revenue as the base year), or at least 2x global GDP. While companies that outsource manufacturing would typically have more stability in gross margins (a trade-off for capped upside), others with their own manufacturing see more variability. Industry margins are a function of manufacturing utilization and inventory supply-demand imbalances. We forecast some companies, especially in analog, to maintain higher inventory levels. Healthy free cash flow generation and high cash levels will likely result in greater shareholder return via share repurchases. Regulatory and geopolitical uncertainties remain notable risks.
